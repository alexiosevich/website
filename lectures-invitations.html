Talk topics

Distances in thin sets

Department colloquium or graduate seminar

Draw a set in the plane and record the distance between each pair of its points. How large must the original set be for these distances to occupy a positive length on the number line? For a finite set one can count points. For a fractal set the natural measure of size is Hausdorff dimension. I will explain Falconer’s question and how Fourier analysis helps turn information about a set into information about its distances.

Joint paper with Larry Guth, Yumeng Ou, and Hong Wang

Signal recovery, restriction theory, and applications

Analysis seminar, applied mathematics seminar, or graduate lecture series

Suppose we transmit the Fourier coefficients of a signal and lose some of them. When do the surviving coefficients determine the signal exactly? I will begin with a finite signal supported on a small set. Counting the missing frequencies gives a first recovery criterion. Their arrangement matters as well. In joint work with Azita Mayeli, we use Fourier restriction estimates to take that arrangement into account.

Short biography

Alex Iosevich is Professor of Mathematics at the University of Rochester. He uses Fourier analysis to study the distances determined by fractal sets and the recovery of signals from incomplete measurements. He founded the StemForAll research program at Rochester. He is a Fellow of the American Mathematical Society and a 2026 Simons Fellow in Mathematics.
