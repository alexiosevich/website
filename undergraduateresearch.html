The central idea

Learn mathematics by helping to create it

StemForAll is a research-apprenticeship program for undergraduate students and especially talented high school students in the Rochester area. It rests on a simple principle: students should have an opportunity to see how research is actually done, even before they have completed every advanced course.

Participants work in small groups on questions drawn from pure and applied mathematics, statistics, computation, physics, biology, and related fields. They learn to formulate problems, read technical material, test ideas, use computation thoughtfully, explain their work, and persist when the answer is not immediate.

The program places learning and the creative process first. Some projects eventually lead to talks, software, or research papers, but the essential goal is to help students become confident and independent thinkers.

The research pathway

How the program works

The summer workshop is the center of the program, but the most productive projects often begin beforehand and continue long afterward.

1

Prepare

Students begin with readings, preliminary exercises, programming, and conversations with project supervisors. The aim is to build the background needed to ask serious questions.

2

Work intensively

The summer workshop combines mini-courses with daily research in small groups. Participants compare approaches, test conjectures, write code when useful, and present what they have learned.

3

Continue

Projects with further potential may continue during the fall and winter. Students can deepen the mathematics and work toward presentations, software, or research papers.
